Why this business?

Prima Innovation takes polyethylene powder, tumbles it inside a heated rotating mould until it fuses into a hollow shape, and sells the result. The output is industrial pallets, insulated boxes and crates for cold chain, plastic road barricades, traffic cones, anti-glare screens and water storage tanks. Manufacturing runs from Pithampur in Madhya Pradesh, which the company's own accounts call its state-of-the-art facility, with a second site at Daman. Domestic sales are 98% of revenue.

The division belonged to Prima Plastics until 31 March 2026, when the NCLT Ahmedabad bench made a demerger scheme effective and every Prima Plastics shareholder received one ₹5 share of Prima Innovation for each ₹10 share held. The stock began trading on 7 August 2026 at around ₹37.05 and closed at ₹34.86 on 12 August after hitting the 5% lower circuit, on volumes measured in hundreds of shares.

Strengths

The margin turn in the last two quarters is genuine and it is measurable. Revenue in the June 2026 quarter was ₹15.30 Cr against ₹12.32 Cr a year earlier, up 24%, and operating margin went from 1.38% to 10.78%. Both quarters are prepared on the same carve-out basis, so the comparison is clean. Strip out the rent and subsidy that sit inside revenue at the FY26 run-rate and core manufacturing margin still improves from roughly 0.5% to roughly 8.9% (estimate — the quarterly filing does not split other operating revenue). That is the first evidence in three years that this asset can make money.

Promoter holding is 58.29% with no pledge, no litigation and a clean CARO report. CNK & Associates signed an unmodified opinion, flagged no key audit matters, found no fraud, no wilful default and no undisclosed income. For a first-year microcap balance sheet, the audit trail is unusually tidy.

Capacity spending is already committed, not merely announced. Capital work in progress rose from ₹0.95 Cr to ₹1.85 Cr during FY26, contracts remaining to be executed on capital account stood at ₹5.38 Cr net of advances, and Kotak Mahindra Bank has sanctioned ₹29.61 Cr. Whatever else is uncertain, the money is arranged.

The subsidy stream is contractual, not discretionary. ₹43.71 lakh came in FY26 under the Madhya Pradesh State Investment Promotion Scheme against capital already invested, with ₹38.04 lakh the prior year. It is small, but it is the kind of income that persists while the plant runs.

Concerns

On a like-for-like full-year basis, revenue did not grow and the loss more than doubled. Screener shows 23% compounded sales growth, which compares Prima Innovation's own first accounting period — 20 June 2024 to 31 March 2025, roughly nine months — against a full twelve months of FY26. The company's Note 52 says outright that the two periods are not comparable. Prima Plastics' Ind AS 105 discontinued-operations note restates both years properly: rotational moulding revenue was ₹51.02 Cr in FY25 and ₹50.97 Cr in FY26, and the loss widened from ₹0.91 Cr to ₹2.03 Cr. Any thesis built on that 23% figure is built on an accounting artefact.

FY26 operating profit was smaller than the non-manufacturing income inside it. Revenue from operations of ₹50.97 Cr includes ₹79.59 lakh of rental income, which was nil the year before, and ₹43.71 lakh of state subsidy. Together that is ₹1.23 Cr against a reported operating profit of ₹0.84 Cr. Core manufacturing generated roughly negative ₹0.39 Cr of EBITDA for the full year. Nothing in the filings explains which asset is being rented out or to whom, and if it is plant capacity then it cuts directly against the expansion story.

Debt service is already at the line and the sanctioned facility pushes it past. The audited ratios show a debt service coverage ratio of 0.97x, down from 2.32x, meaning cash generation did not cover interest plus scheduled principal. Cash interest cover was 0.71x. Total borrowings are ₹25.39 Cr against ₹26.58 Cr of equity, and ₹11.39 Cr of that debt is a loan from Prima Plastics rather than a bank. If the full ₹29.61 Cr Kotak facility is drawn on top of the parent loan, debt reaches roughly ₹41 Cr against unchanged equity, taking D/E to about 1.54x. Prima Plastics has put a special resolution to its members to guarantee ₹28.86 Cr of that ₹29.61 Cr facility, which says the lender would not extend the credit against Prima Innovation's own balance sheet. Prima Plastics carries a CRISIL BBB+/Stable rating on ₹60 Cr of facilities; Prima Innovation is unrated.

Reported operating cash flow depends on a working-capital swing that cannot repeat indefinitely. FY26 cash from operations was ₹4.12 Cr, of which a ₹4.87 Cr increase in other liabilities did the heavy lifting. Operating profit before working-capital changes was ₹0.94 Cr. Inventory rose 47% to ₹13.55 Cr while revenue was flat, receivables sat at 114 days, and closing cash was ₹3.27 lakh across the whole company.

Separation from the parent is not finished. At 31 March 2026 the title deeds to every immovable property — the MP factory at ₹14.14 Cr gross, the Daman factory at ₹3.78 Cr, three Daman apartments and freehold Daman land — were still registered in Prima Plastics' name, with transfer described as in process. The gratuity fund at LIC is still in the parent's name. Independent directors, the CFO and the company secretary were all appointed on 24 March 2026, a week before the scheme took effect.

How does this actually compound?

It does not, on the evidence available. Return on capital employed was −2.08% in FY26 and return on equity −7.35%. A business that earns less than nothing on capital destroys value with every rupee it reinvests, so the reinvestment-rate arithmetic that drives the multi-bagger framework has no positive input to work with. The question that matters is whether the June-quarter margin marks a permanent change in that arithmetic. Full breakdown in Section 3.

What does the market think — and where do I disagree?

Four trading days is not long enough for a consensus view to form. There is no analyst coverage, no investor presentation, no earnings call, and no institutional holding disclosed in the shareholding pattern. Phase 4.5.4 asks for the market's label and a catalyst that flips it; here there is no label yet to flip.

What the price does say is arithmetically specific. Market cap of ₹38.4 Cr plus net debt of ₹25.4 Cr gives an enterprise value of ₹63.7 Cr. Compounded forward at a 14% required return for three years, that is ₹94 Cr of enterprise value in FY29, which at 8x EV/EBITDA needs ₹11.8 Cr of EBITDA. Prima Innovation's trailing twelve-month EBITDA is ₹0.84 Cr and the annualised June-quarter figure is ₹6.6 Cr. So today's price already requires the capacity expansion to work, margins to hold near 12%, and revenue to reach roughly ₹95 Cr. That is the bull case in the table below, being paid for in advance.

Second-Order Stress Test

Five-Whys on the ROIC engine

Why does this business earn negative returns on capital? Because FY26 operating profit of ₹0.84 Cr sat on ₹52 Cr of capital employed, and after depreciation the operating result was a loss.

Why is operating margin so thin? Materials consumed take 58.9% of revenue, power and fuel another 8.0%, outsourced labour charges 6.9%, and freight 4.6%. By the time those four lines are paid, 78% of every rupee is gone before employees and overhead.

Why can't the company price above that cost base? Rotomoulded pallets, insulated crates, cones and barricades are made to standard dimensions by many converters. A buyer comparing two suppliers of a 1200x1000 pallet is comparing price and delivery.

Why are there so many converters? Rotational moulding machinery is cheap next to injection moulding, minimum efficient scale is low, and the moulds are simple. A regional player with two lines can enter and compete.

Why does that persist? Nothing in the industry structure removes it. There is no licence, no certification barrier, no network density and no switching cost embedded in the customer's process. The only durable advantage available is freight cost, which lets a plant undercut distant rivals within a few hundred kilometres and nothing beyond that.

The fifth answer is structural, which the framework normally treats as a good sign. Here it points the wrong way: the structure of the industry is what caps returns, and no amount of execution changes it at ₹50 Cr of revenue.

Margin behaviour through stress (Phase 4.5.3)

Phase 4.5.3 allows three valid states for a business whose margins have been compressed. Prima Innovation's case would have to be state 3, margins temporarily absorbed by capex, which the framework permits only when the prior capex cycle converted at ROIC above 20%.

The prior cycle is on the record. Prima Plastics added a fifth unit at Pithampur in 2022. Rotational moulding revenue reached ₹51.02 Cr by FY25 and ₹50.97 Cr in FY26, and the division delivered −2.08% ROCE and a widening loss. The prior cycle did not convert. Under the framework's own wording, current margin absorption is therefore indistinguishable from value destruction, and that disqualifies the setup even at a low valuation.

This is the single check that sets the grade. Without it the note lands at Grade C with a speculative sizing; with it, Grade D.

World-state under the base case

Two years out (FY28). Revenue around ₹70 Cr at 9-10% operating margin, with the Kotak facility drawn and depreciation and interest both roughly ₹1.3-1.5 Cr per year higher than today. Net profit is close to breakeven because the new fixed costs absorb most of the incremental gross profit. Management's agenda is dominated by things this team has not done as a standalone company: filling a plant it has just enlarged, servicing bank debt without a parent guarantee, and running listed-company compliance on a ₹5 Cr employee cost base. The risk success creates is that a full order book invites the two large peers to price against them in the same regions.

Five years out (FY31). Either the expanded Pithampur capacity found customers at a margin above 11%, in which case revenue is ₹110-130 Cr and the equity is worth several times today's, or utilisation stalled and the company is carrying ₹40 Cr of debt against ₹25 Cr of equity with a fixed-cost base sized for volume that never arrived. Because interest and depreciation on the new capacity are fixed while volumes are not, outcomes between those two cases are narrow.

Market label vs reality (Phase 4.5.4)

No established label exists after four trading days. The observable supply dynamic is that Prima Plastics holders who wanted a dividend-paying furniture maker received shares in a loss-making roto business and some are selling, which is a reason the price is falling but not a reason it is mispriced. Naming a label-change catalyst is not possible yet, and that absence is itself a reason to wait rather than to act.

Downside Framework — What Protects Capital?

Very little. Net block plus right-of-use assets is ₹30.7 Cr against ₹25.4 Cr of debt, so the asset backing net of debt is roughly ₹5 Cr, or ₹4.8 per share, against a ₹34.86 price. Book value per share of ₹24.16 looks like a floor until you note that the return on that book was −7.35%. A plant that earns less than its cost of capital is worth less to a buyer than its carrying value, which is why loss-making manufacturers routinely trade below book rather than at it. There is no cash buffer — ₹3.27 lakh at year end. The one genuine backstop is the promoter family's willingness to fund the business, evidenced by the ₹11.39 Cr loan and the proposed ₹28.86 Cr guarantee, and that is support for the lenders rather than for minority shareholders.

Detailed Analysis

Deep Dives

A. Why Screener's growth figures are wrong for this company, and what the real numbers are

Screener reports 23% compounded sales growth and −1,745% compounded profit growth for Prima Innovation. Both are artefacts and neither should enter a thesis.

Prima Innovation was incorporated on 20 June 2024 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of Prima Plastics. Its first accounting period ran from that date to 31 March 2025, about nine months. The demerger scheme set an appointed date of 1 July 2024, so the rotational moulding business is reflected in that stub period from July 2024 onward. FY26 is a full twelve months. Comparing ₹41.45 Cr over nine months with ₹50.97 Cr over twelve produces 23% growth from arithmetic alone. Note 52 of the audited accounts states the periods are not comparable, and the auditor's Emphasis of Matter explains that Ind AS 103 would have required accounting from the date of control — 31 March 2026 — but the scheme and MCA Circular 09/2019 dated 21 August 2019 permitted the appointed date instead.

The −1,745% profit figure comes from the same source compounded by the share count. Reported EPS of −₹1,013.90 for FY26 and −₹56.61 for FY25 are computed on 20,000 shares, the pre-allotment capital. There are now 1,10,00,470 shares.

The clean comparison exists because Prima Plastics had to present the division as a discontinued operation under Ind AS 105 and restate both years on the same basis. Note 52 of the parent's FY26 annual report gives revenue from operations of ₹51.02 Cr in FY25 against ₹50.97 Cr in FY26, with the loss after tax widening from ₹0.91 Cr to ₹2.03 Cr. That is the like-for-like record: no growth, and a loss that more than doubled.

The practical consequence is that any screen or platform reading Prima Innovation's headline growth will rank it as a fast grower. It is not.

B. What actually improved in the last two quarters, and how much of it is real

The reported change is large. Operating margin went from 1.38% in the June 2025 quarter to 10.12% in the March 2026 quarter and 10.78% in June 2026, on revenue rising from ₹12.32 Cr to ₹15.30 Cr.

Two adjustments are needed before reading that as an operating turn.

First, FY26 revenue from operations contains ₹79.59 lakh of rental income that did not exist in FY25 and ₹43.71 lakh of Madhya Pradesh state subsidy. Spread evenly that is roughly ₹31 lakh a quarter, against zero rent in the June 2025 quarter when only the subsidy applied. Removing it takes the June 2025 quarter to roughly 0.5% core margin and the June 2026 quarter to roughly 8.9%. The improvement survives the adjustment and remains large.

Second, the entire FY25 and FY26 cost base was incurred by Prima Plastics on Prima Innovation's behalf, which the related-party note states explicitly. Key managerial remuneration in FY26 was ₹1.61 lakh in total because the people running the division were on the parent's payroll. From FY27 the company carries its own CFO, company secretary, four independent directors, statutory audit, listing and depository fees. That is a new annual cost, probably ₹40-60 lakh, that has not yet appeared in a full year of results and will land against the improved margin rather than on top of it.

What plausibly drove the improvement itself is not disclosed. Materials at 58.9% of revenue is the largest single line, and polymer prices softened through 2025-26, which would show up exactly this way. If the margin gain is input-cost driven it reverses when polymer prices turn. If it is mix — more insulated crates and fewer commodity pallets — it holds. The filings do not say, and there is no earnings call to ask on.

C. The debt picture after the Kotak drawdown

Borrowings at 31 March 2026 were ₹25.39 Cr, split ₹11.44 Cr non-current and ₹13.95 Cr current. Of that total, ₹11.39 Cr is a loan from Prima Plastics recorded as payable pursuant to the demerger, which the parent carries in its own books as an unsecured loan to a related party. Bank debt is therefore about ₹14 Cr.

Prima Plastics' AGM notice discloses that Prima Innovation has obtained a ₹29.61 Cr facility from Kotak Mahindra Bank to fund capacity expansion, and asks members to approve a corporate guarantee of up to ₹28.86 Cr in its favour under Section 186. If drawn in full alongside the parent loan, total debt reaches roughly ₹41 Cr against ₹26.58 Cr of equity, a debt-to-equity ratio near 1.54x. Phase 0.2 of the framework treats anything above 1.5x as a concern for a non-financial, non-infrastructure business.

The guarantee itself carries information beyond the number. A bank lending ₹29.61 Cr to a company with ₹26.58 Cr of equity, a 0.97x debt service coverage ratio and two loss years wants a guarantor, and the guarantor is the listed parent that Prima Innovation was separated from four months earlier. Prima Plastics holds a CRISIL BBB+/Stable rating on ₹60 Cr of bank facilities from Kotak and Axis, reaffirmed on 28 July 2026 and removed from rating watch after the demerger completed. Prima Innovation has no rating of its own.

For Prima Plastics shareholders, who are the same people, the guarantee means the separation is incomplete in economic terms: the parent's balance sheet still stands behind the demerged business.

1. Business Summary

Prima Innovation Limited manufactures rotationally moulded plastic products. The process heats polyethylene powder inside a mould that rotates on two axes until the material fuses to the mould wall, producing seamless hollow items with uniform wall thickness. It suits large, low-volume, hollow shapes where injection moulding would need prohibitively expensive tooling.

The product range covers industrial pallets, insulated boxes and crates used in cold chain and fisheries, plastic road barricades, traffic cones, traffic fences, anti-glare screens for highway medians, and water storage tanks. End markets are therefore split between industrial material handling, cold chain logistics, and road infrastructure, the last of which tends to be contractor and tender driven.

Manufacturing runs from Pithampur in Dhar district, Madhya Pradesh, described in the company's own Note 1 as its state-of-the-art facility, and from Daman, where the registered office also sits. Gross carrying value of the MP factory land and building is ₹14.14 Cr against ₹3.78 Cr for Daman, so Pithampur is the primary site.

Revenue was ₹50.97 Cr in FY26, of which exports were ₹0.92 Cr, or 1.85%. The company reports a single business segment and a single geographical segment.

The business was a division of Prima Plastics Limited, incorporated 1993, until the NCLT Ahmedabad bench sanctioned a demerger scheme on 16 March 2026. Prima Plastics retains moulded furniture, injection-moulded waste management bins, and overseas interests including a Cameroon joint venture and a Latin American subsidiary.

3. Compounding Engine — Q&A

Q0. Operating Leverage Test — does growth create margin leverage?

Over the full year, no. Revenue was flat at ₹51 Cr across FY25 and FY26 and margins fell, which is the opposite of leverage. Within the last two quarters, yes, and clearly: revenue rose 24% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 while operating margin rose 940 basis points, so profit grew far faster than sales. Both statements are true and they describe different periods. Which one describes the business depends on whether the quarterly pattern holds, which two prints cannot establish.

Q1. Is incremental ROIC genuinely high (>20%) — and structural, not cyclical?

No. The three-year incremental calculation the framework asks for cannot be run because the company has only two reporting periods, one of them a stub. The available substitute is the parent's record for the same asset: capital was added at Pithampur in 2022 and the division delivered −2.08% ROCE in FY26 with a widening loss. On the current year alone, NOPAT is negative, so incremental ROIC is negative regardless of the denominator.

Q2. How much capital can be redeployed at these returns — the reinvestment runway?

The reinvestment-rate formula requires positive NOPAT and there is none. What can be said is that the company intends to deploy ₹29.61 Cr of new bank borrowing against a ₹26.58 Cr equity base and a ₹30.7 Cr net block, which is close to a doubling of invested capital. At the returns demonstrated so far, doubling invested capital doubles the value destruction; at the returns implied by the June quarter, the same spending pays for itself. Which of those applies is the whole question, and two quarters of data cannot answer it.

Q3. What's the implied compounding math?

Taking the June quarter at face value and annualising gives revenue of ₹61.2 Cr, EBITDA of ₹6.6 Cr and PAT of ₹1.84 Cr on capital employed of about ₹52 Cr. After depreciation of roughly ₹2.0 Cr that is operating profit of ₹4.6 Cr, or 8.8% return on capital employed, which sits below the 12-15% cost of equity for Indian small caps and well below the 20% the framework requires before a business qualifies as a compounder. Extrapolating the best quarter available gets the company to the point of covering its capital costs, not past it.

Q4. What breaks the thesis?

There is no bull thesis here to break. What would break the AVOID is set out in the Action section: two more quarters above 8% core margin, FY27 revenue above ₹65 Cr on volume, debt service coverage above 1.5x with a disclosed capacity plan, and completed title transfer.

Glossary — Key Industry Terms

Rotational moulding (rotomoulding). Plastic powder is loaded into a hollow metal mould, which is heated in an oven while rotating on two axes. The powder melts and coats the inside of the mould evenly, then the mould is cooled and opened. It produces seamless hollow parts — tanks, bins, pallets, cones — with even wall thickness. It suits large parts made in modest quantities because the moulds are far cheaper than injection-moulding tools, but each cycle is slow and energy-intensive.

Injection moulding. Molten plastic is forced under pressure into a closed steel tool. Faster per part and better for detail, but the tooling costs many times more, so it needs high volumes to pay back. Prima Plastics kept this business; Prima Innovation got the rotomoulding.

Insulated crate / box. A rotomoulded container with a layer of foam sealed between two plastic walls, used to keep fish, produce, ice cream or vaccines cold in transit without mechanical refrigeration.

Anti-glare screen. Plastic fins fixed along a highway median to block oncoming headlight glare. Sold to road contractors, so demand follows highway construction.

Demerger / scheme of arrangement. A court-approved corporate restructuring that carves a division out of a company into a separate legal entity, with the parent's shareholders receiving shares in the new entity directly. Here every Prima Plastics shareholder received one ₹5 Prima Innovation share for each ₹10 Prima Plastics share.

Appointed date vs effective date. The appointed date (1 July 2024) is the date from which the transferred business is treated as belonging to the new company for accounting. The effective date (31 March 2026) is when the scheme legally took effect. The gap is why Prima Innovation's first accounts show a business it did not legally own for most of the period.

Carve-out accounting. Financial statements built for a division that was never a separate company, by identifying its direct revenues, costs, assets and liabilities and allocating shared items on a stated basis. Reliable for direction but not identical to how the business would have looked as a standalone.

Discontinued operations (Ind AS 105). When a company disposes of a major business line, it must show that line separately in the profit and loss account and restate the prior year the same way, so readers can compare like with like. Prima Plastics' compliance with this rule is what made the honest two-year comparison of the rotomoulding business available.

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR). Cash available to service debt divided by interest plus scheduled principal repayment. Below 1.0x means the business did not generate enough cash to meet its debt obligations from operations and had to find the difference elsewhere. Prima Innovation's was 0.97x in FY26.

Interest coverage. Operating profit divided by interest cost. It answers how many times over the business can pay its interest bill from operations. Below 1x means it cannot. The framework treats below 3x as a concern.

Corporate guarantee. A promise by one company to repay another company's loan if that company defaults. Lenders ask for one when the borrower's own balance sheet does not support the loan. Prima Plastics is proposing to guarantee ₹28.86 Cr of Prima Innovation's ₹29.61 Cr Kotak facility.

Capital work in progress (CWIP). Money already spent on plant and equipment that is not yet finished or operating, so it earns nothing until commissioned. Rising CWIP signals capacity being built.

Free float. Shares not held by promoters, which is what can actually trade. Prima Innovation's is about 45.9 lakh shares, roughly ₹16 Cr at the current price.

Lower circuit. An exchange-set limit on how far a stock may fall in a single session. Prima Innovation closed at its 5% limit on 12 August 2026, meaning sellers remained at the floor price with no buyers.
