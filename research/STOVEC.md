## Summary Verdict

### Why this business?

Stovec makes the consumable "screens" and related chemistry that textile mills use to print patterns onto fabric — nickel rotary screens, lacquers, and the graphics/galvanic products around them — plus it distributes and services its Dutch parent's printing machinery in India. The bet a value investor might be tempted by is the surface cheapness against history: this was a 40%+ ROCE, debt-free, high-dividend niche monopoly-ish supplier that earned ₹39 Cr on ₹193 Cr of sales in 2018. The problem is that the engine has quietly broken. Revenue has gone nowhere in seven years (₹193 Cr in 2018, ₹198 Cr in 2025), operating margin has collapsed from 20% to 5%, and profit is down to ₹7 Cr. What you are really being asked to buy at ₹1,700 is a structurally lower-margin cyclical whose upside in a good year is capped by a parent that takes an ever-larger cut through royalty and dividends. There is no compounding here to underwrite.

### Strengths

- **Fortress balance sheet.** Zero borrowings every single year 2014-2025; ₹132 Cr of net worth (book value ₹631/share) against a ₹355 Cr market cap. No bankruptcy risk, ever.
- **Pre-existing capability that is real:** an established rotary-screen manufacturing base in Ahmedabad plus the exclusive India distribution/service rights and technology access to SPGPrints — the physical asset (plant + parent tech pipeline) exists and generated 40-49% ROCE as recently as 2015-2017, so the earning power is proven, not hypothetical.
- **Committed, non-selling parent:** SPGPrints B.V. has held 71.06% flat for a decade with zero pledge; a global strategic owner is not walking away from its India platform.
- **Genuine minority-holder protection on display:** public shareholders successfully blocked the parent's IPR-purchase (93.85% against) and material-RPT (86.91% against) resolutions at the May 2026 AGM — the governance guardrail actually functioned.

### Concerns

- **Structural, not cyclical, margin collapse.** OPM ran 17-21% through 2019 and now sits at 5% (FY2025) with the Dec-2025 quarter at 1.03% OPM and ₹0.16 Cr PAT. Seven years of flat revenue at a permanently lower margin is the profile of a business that has lost pricing power to competition and input costs, not one waiting on a cycle.
- **The parent is a value-extraction mechanism, and it is accelerating.** Royalty to SPGPrints has compounded at ~133% CAGR; the parent tried to push through an IPR acquisition and an expanded RPT mandate at the 2026 AGM. Minorities won this round, but a 71%-owner that keeps returning to the well caps how much of any recovery ever reaches the float. Related-party outflow plus heavy dividends have run the reserve base down from ₹172 Cr (2022) to ₹130 Cr (2025).
- **Cash generation has turned negative.** Operating cash flow was ₹-2 Cr in FY2025 — the first negative year in the dataset — while inventory days ballooned to 182 (from ~110-130 historically). Unsold stock is piling up as textile demand stays soft.
- **Priced for a recovery that isn't visible.** 2.7x book and ~37x the trailing three-year average PAT for a no-growth, margin-impaired cyclical leaves no margin of safety; the base-case fair value sits roughly half the current price.

### How does this actually compound? (short answer)

It doesn't, currently. The compounding engine — high ROIC reinvested into a growing textile-print market — has stalled: the market isn't growing for Stovec (flat revenue since 2018), incremental ROIC over the last three years is negative (NOPAT fell while capital was broadly flat), and the parent siphons a rising share of what is earned. This is a cash cow being milked, not a reinvestment story.

### What does the market think — and where do I disagree?

At ₹1,700 the market is implicitly valuing Stovec on mid-cycle "normalised" earnings and betting the 2015-2019 margin structure returns — a P/E of 59x on ₹7 Cr trough profit only makes sense if you believe profit snaps back to ₹25-40 Cr. I disagree that it snaps back structurally: margins have been sub-10% for three consecutive years (2023-2025), the decline predates any single cyclical shock, and each recovery attempt (Q1-2026 clawed OPM back only to 5.7%) is capped by a rising royalty charge. My normalised estimate is EPS of ₹60-70 in a decent year, not ₹120+, which puts fair value near ₹850-950 — a ~45% gap below CMP, on the wrong side.

### Second-Order Stress Test

**5-Whys on the (former) ROIC engine — why did it break?**
> 1. ROCE fell from ~45% to ~8%. Why? → Operating margin collapsed from 20% to 5% while sales stayed flat.
> 2. Why did margin collapse? → Lost pricing power in rotary screens; input and competitive pressure with no volume growth to absorb fixed costs.
> 3. Why the lost pricing power? → Rotary screen printing is a mature, slow-growing textile technology facing digital-printing substitution and low-cost competition; Stovec's niche is no longer scarce.
> 4. Why can't the parent fix it? → The parent's incentive is to extract via royalty/IPR/dividend, not to reinvest India profits into share gain — every 2026 AGM resolution pointed toward extraction.
> 5. Structural root: **a shrinking-to-flat addressable technology plus a parent whose interests diverge from minority holders.** Both are structural and adverse — this is the opposite of a durable moat.

**The world in 2 years if the "recovery" base case plays out:** revenue perhaps ₹210-220 Cr, OPM clawing to 8-10%, PAT ₹18-22 Cr — a return to 2024 levels, not new highs. Even there, the stock at ₹1,700 would trade ~17-19x with no growth beyond, and the parent's royalty take would be higher. Management's agenda is defensive: defend margin, manage the parent relationship, keep paying the dividend.

**What success itself triggers:** any genuine profit recovery hands the parent a stronger argument to re-table the RPT/royalty/IPR resolutions it lost in 2026 — success flows disproportionately to SPGPrints, not the minority.

# Detailed Analysis

## 1. Business Summary

Stovec Industries (Ahmedabad, incorporated 1973) is a subsidiary of SPGPrints B.V. of the Netherlands (71.06%). It operates in three areas: rotary screen printing consumables (nickel rotary screens, lacquers and chemistry used by textile mills to print patterns on fabric — the historical core), graphics products (screens and equipment for graphic/industrial printing), and galvanic products (electroformed metal parts). It also acts as the Indian distribution and service arm for SPGPrints' textile-printing machinery. The business is cyclical, geared to textile-industry capex and mill activity, and its technology comes from the parent in exchange for royalty. Revenue has been flat around ₹200-235 Cr since 2021 while profitability has structurally weakened.

## Glossary — Key Industry Terms

**Rotary screen printing**
A textile-printing method where the pattern is etched onto a thin cylindrical ("rotary") nickel mesh screen; as fabric passes under the rotating cylinder, dye is pushed through the open areas of the mesh to print the design, one screen per colour. The screens are consumables that wear out and are re-ordered — this recurring consumable demand is Stovec's historical core. The technology is mature and increasingly challenged by digital textile printing (inkjet directly onto fabric), which needs no screens.

**Galvanic / galvano products**
Parts made by electroforming — depositing metal (typically nickel) onto a mould through an electrolytic bath to build a precise thin metal shell. It is the same electroplating chemistry used to make the rotary screens themselves, extended to other precision metal components.

**SPGPrints B.V.**
The Dutch parent, a global supplier of textile and industrial printing systems (rotary screen and digital), which owns 71.06% of Stovec. It supplies Stovec with product technology and machinery to distribute in India, and in return receives royalty payments and dividends. The relationship is the central governance question: the parent's incentive is to maximise extraction from the Indian subsidiary (royalty, IP transfers, dividends), which can conflict with minority shareholders — as seen when Stovec's public holders voted down two parent-favouring resolutions at the May 2026 AGM.

**Royalty (in an MNC-subsidiary context)**
A fee the Indian subsidiary pays the parent for using the parent's technology/brand, usually a percentage of sales. It is a legitimate cost, but because it is paid to the controlling shareholder it is a related-party transaction — a high or fast-rising royalty transfers profit from all shareholders (including minorities) to the parent alone. Stovec's royalty has reportedly compounded at ~133% CAGR, which is why minority holders scrutinise it.
