## Deep-research update (2026-08-23) — a TNT-and-munitions programme is in the accounts and the regulator's file, the environmental clearance is NOT granted, and the ICRA downgrade was an agency switch

Four things changed since the 9 August note, and they do not all point the same way.

### Direct answer on the environmental clearance: it has not been granted

Keltech applied to expand the Garamsur plant (Village Garamsur, Post Dudhala, Tehsil Katol, District Nagpur) under MoEF&CC file **IA-J-11011/360/2018-IA-II(I)**, Category A, Sector 5(f), appraised by EAC (Industry-III). The file has moved through the early stages and then stopped, at least as far as the public record shows:

| Stage | Status | Reference | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing EC (the sanctioned baseline) | **Granted** | F. No. IA-J-11011/360/2018-IA II (I) | 14 Jul 2020 |
| Existing Consent to Operate (MPCB) | Valid to 31 Mar 2028 | MPCBCONSENT-0000183363/CO/2401000860 | 6 Jan 2024 |
| Terms of Reference for the expansion | **Granted** | Same file number | 21 Sep 2024 |
| Draft EIA/EMP | Submitted | Anacon Laboratories, ANqr/PD/20A/2024/244 | Jan 2025 |
| Public hearing | **Conducted** | mpcb.gov.in/en/node/6917 | 17 May 2025 |
| EAC appraisal after the hearing | **Not found** | — | — |
| **Final environmental clearance** | **No evidence of grant** | — | — |

So the position is a granted Terms of Reference and a completed public hearing, with no final clearance located. Absence of an EC letter in the public record is not proof of refusal, and the hearing minutes themselves (`MoM_of_Keltech_Eng.pdf`) are listed on the MPCB page but returned 404 on every retrieval path, so objections raised at the hearing are unknown. What can be said is that **the expansion cannot lawfully operate at the applied-for capacities until an EC issues, and no such grant is on the record.** Anyone treating "environmental clearance" as a completed catalyst is pricing a stage the file has not reached.

### What the application actually covers — far more than a TNT line

The EIA capacities are the most valuable thing in this update, because they show the intended shape of the business rather than the current one. Against an existing sanction that permits only 60 MT of TNT in storage and no TNT manufacture at all, the application seeks:

| Product | Existing sanction | Proposed | Total after expansion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TNT** | nil (60 MT storage only) | **+4,000 MTPA** | 4,000 MTPA |
| **HMX / HNS / RDX** | nil | **+1,000 MTPA** | 1,000 MTPA |
| Nitrocellulose | nil | +500 MTPA | 500 MTPA |
| Nitroglycerine | nil | +500 MTPA | 500 MTPA |
| PETN | 1,600 MTPA | +3,200 MTPA | 4,800 MTPA |
| SME bulk (no EC needed) | 20,000 MTPA | +20,000 MTPA | 40,000 MTPA |

And, under a heading the EIA titles **"Explosives for Defense (Domestic & Export)"**, five finished-munition lines at 100,000 units per annum each: **155mm and 105mm artillery shells, and 120mm, 81mm and 82mm mortar bombs**. Project cost in the EIA is ₹36.4 Cr existing plus **₹100 Cr additional**, and manpower rises from 256 to 802.

RDX and HMX are the high explosives that fill warheads; artillery shells and mortar bombs are ordnance, not mining consumables. A company that builds this is a defence-energetics company. **The governing caveat is that this is what Keltech has applied for.** Only the TNT line appears in the audited accounts as under construction. Nothing in the munitions scope is approved, financed, or disclosed as being built, and no MoD licence, contract, order or offtake exists anywhere in the public record.

### Correction to the 9 August note — the ICRA downgrade was a mandate switch, not a credit event

The 9 August update treated ICRA's 23 February 2026 downgrade to BB+ / Issuer Not Cooperating as the single most important development and read it as a hard governance signal. That reading was too harsh, and the reason is a filing the earlier pass missed: **CRISIL assigned BBB+/Stable and A2 to ₹258 Cr of Keltech's bank facilities on 13 February 2026 — ten days before the ICRA action** — disclosed to BSE under Regulation 30 on the same day with the rating letter annexed, and still outstanding on CRISIL's website. The sequence reads as Keltech moving its rating mandate from ICRA to CRISIL, after which ICRA downgraded the now-orphaned facilities for unpaid surveillance fees and lack of information.

The credit read is therefore benign, and better than before: **₹258 Cr rated at BBB+/A2, including a ₹163 Cr Canara Bank term loan** — which is the best-sourced measure of the sanctioned capex envelope, against only about ₹49 Cr of long-term debt drawn at 31 March 2026. Most of the facility is undrawn headroom.

Two things survive the correction. The company let a live rating decay to BB+ INC rather than pay a fee or formally withdraw it, which is a housekeeping signal even if not a credit one. And CRISIL's rating *rationale*, as distinct from the letter, could not be retrieved, so **no obtainable rating document from either agency mentions TNT, munitions or defence at all** — the agencies appear not to have been told, or not to have commented.

### On "Thriveni" — a real operational adjacency, not a disclosed relationship

The FY26 CWIP note names a **"Surjagarh Silo set-up"** alongside the TNT project. Surjagarh is the Gadchiroli iron ore mine held by Lloyds Metals & Energy, where **Thriveni Earthmovers has been Mine Developer and Operator since 2021**. Keltech is therefore capitalising a bulk-explosives silo at a mine Thriveni runs, which is a genuine operational adjacency — silos are built to serve blasting at one specific mine.

It stops there. The strings "Thriveni" and "Thrive" appear **zero times** in the FY26 annual report, including in related-party disclosures and the above-5% shareholder table; there is no order, contract or MoU announcement of any kind in the 2026 BSE stream; and the counterparty for the silo could equally be Lloyds Metals directly. Treat the connection as an inference from third-party sources, not as a disclosed customer relationship.

### The rest of the update

Two further things changed, and they point in opposite directions.

**Correction first, because it goes against the previous call.** The 9 August update said the FY26 annual report "disclosed no defence segment and no order book." The segment statement is still literally true — the reporting segments remain Explosives and Perlite — but it was the wrong place to look, and the conclusion drawn from it was too strong. The capital-work-in-progress note on page 103 of the same annual report says, in one line: *"Capital work-in-progress mainly comprises of TNT Project at Garamsur, Surjagarh Silo set-up."* Note 13(a) then shows a dedicated Canara Bank term loan **"Towards TNT Project"** at **8.50%**, drawn to **₹20.45 Cr** at 31 March 2026 against nil the prior year, secured on plant and machinery at Garamsur. TNT is a military explosive. Keltech is building a TNT plant, it has project debt against it, and I read past it.

The auditor made the capex programme a **Key Audit Matter**: capacity expansion at both plant locations, with **₹55.41 Cr of assets put to use** during FY26 and **₹38.01 Cr still in construction**. Capital commitments contracted but not yet spent doubled to **₹39.96 Cr**. Borrowing cost capitalised jumped from ₹0.17 Cr to **₹1.77 Cr**. On 7 August the AGM passed a special resolution lifting the borrowing ceiling under section 180(1)(c) to **₹400 Cr**, against total debt of ₹79 Cr today. This is a company in the middle of a capex cycle roughly a quarter the size of its revenue, financed with debt, and it discusses none of it in the Management Discussion and Analysis, which mentions neither TNT nor defence and offers only "New plant commissioned in March 2026 is expected to stabilise."

**The second change is that the exchange has intervened.** Keltech is now under **BSE's Enhanced Surveillance Measure, Stage 2**. That means trade-for-trade settlement, a **2% price band**, **100% margin** on every position, and **periodic call auction** instead of continuous trading — the 20 August tape showed open, high, low and close all at ₹13,601 on a single auction print. ESM Stage 2 runs for a minimum of one month and a security only steps down to Stage 1 if its close-to-close variation stays under 8% over that month.

### What this does to each of the two lenses

**Fundamental (compounding) read: AVOID, and the price has made it worse, not better.** The stock closed **₹14,250 on 21 August**, up 46% since 7 August and **+184% from the ₹5,010 AVOID call on 1 June**. Market cap is ₹1,425 Cr against FY26 revenue of ₹532 Cr and underlying PAT of about ₹26.3 Cr, so this is roughly **54x underlying earnings and 9.3x book** for a business that earned a 7.68% operating margin in FY26 and 7.53% in the June quarter. A TNT and munitions programme is a real option on a defence-grade product mix and deserves to be priced as one. It is being priced at a defence multiple before a rupee of TNT revenue, before the environmental clearance that the capacities depend on has been granted, before any MoD licence or offtake exists, and before the plant is commissioned.

Worth stating plainly because it bears on the flow: **BSE carries no announcement of any kind between 7 and 21 August.** No TNT disclosure, no defence order, no EC receipt, no capex statement — nothing that explains a 46% move. The company's only price-movement clarification this year, filed 25 April, said the move "is purely market driven and may be combination of various factors including market conditions which the Company neither has control nor has any knowledge of reasons." Every material fact in this update — the TNT project, the ₹163 Cr term loan, the munitions application — was disclosed passively, in an annual-report footnote, a rating annexure and a regulator portal. None was announced as price-sensitive information under Regulation 30.

**Swing-trade read: the setup is now mechanically un-tradeable, which is a stronger statement than "decayed."** The 9 August note said the window had closed because the catalyst had passed. ESM Stage 2 goes further. A 2% band with 100% margin and periodic call auction removes the leveraged, continuous-quote flow that drove the re-rating in the first place, and it removes the exit. If the theme cools, the path down is a queue of 2% lower-circuit auctions with no buyer on the other side, and a position cannot be unwound at any price the screen shows. The lesson note below argued for a bounded speculative trade in thin-float thematic names; that argument does not survive an ESM Stage 2 tag, and any future application of it should treat surveillance status as a hard gate before sizing.

### Q1 FY27 re-cut against the primary filing — margin compressed, and the capex is already eating the growth

The 31 July filing shows the June quarter more sharply than the earlier summary did. Revenue was **₹183.28 Cr against ₹143.03 Cr**, up 28.1%. Operating profit went from ₹12.11 Cr to ₹13.80 Cr, so **operating margin fell from 8.47% to 7.53%**, a 94 basis point compression rather than the "flat 7.5%" recorded earlier. Below that line, finance costs rose 72% to ₹1.58 Cr and depreciation rose 29% to ₹2.34 Cr as the March-2026 plant came on. Those two lines together added ₹1.18 Cr of fixed cost against ₹1.69 Cr of incremental operating profit, which is why a 28% revenue jump produced a 2.3% rise in pre-tax profit and a **1.6% fall in net profit** to ₹8.08 Cr.

With ₹38.01 Cr still in construction and ₹39.96 Cr contracted on top, the depreciation and interest drag grows from here while the TNT plant contributes nothing until it is commissioned. That is the arithmetic the 52x multiple has to survive.

### FY26 profit is flattered by a non-cash duty write-back

Note 40 discloses that the **₹3.18 Cr exceptional item** in FY26 is a reversal of anti-dumping duty on ammonium nitrate imports, charged to profit back in FY2020-21 after a Directorate of Revenue Intelligence inquiry. CESTAT set aside the lower authority's order in April 2026, and the company has written the amount back as exceptional income and parked it as a "Duty Receivable" under other financial assets. **No cash has been received** — the refund application is still being filed. Reported FY26 PAT of ₹28.66 Cr therefore contains ₹3.18 Cr of non-cash write-back; taxing the pre-exceptional profit at the same effective 26.2% gives **underlying PAT of about ₹26.3 Cr**, which is the number the valuation above uses. The wider dispute is worth noting on its own: the Commissioner of Customs had levied ₹13.75 Cr in FY2024-25 on the same matter, which Keltech appealed and won.

### Revised multi-bagger math at ₹14,250

The capex programme is real, so the FY29 estimates below give it credit. Explosives assets turn at about 1.5x revenue-to-assets for this company, so a ₹133 Cr programme (₹93 Cr deployed plus ₹40 Cr committed) supports roughly ₹200 Cr of incremental revenue at maturity.

| Scenario | FY29 Revenue | OPM | PAT | EPS | Exit multiple | Value | vs ₹14,250 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear — capex underdelivers, mix unchanged | ₹620 Cr | 7.5% | ₹19.6 Cr | ₹196 | 15x | **₹2,940** | **−79%** |
| Base — capex fills, modest mix gain | ₹780 Cr | 8.5% | ₹32.8 Cr | ₹328 | 20x | **₹6,560** | **−54%** |
| Bull — TNT commissioned and winning defence offtake | ₹900 Cr | 12% | ₹61.4 Cr | ₹614 | 30x | **₹18,420** | **+29%** |

- **Margin of safety vs base case: (6,560 − 14,250) / 6,560 = −117%.** The stock trades at well over twice base-case fair value.
- **Downside to bear: −79%.**
- **Bull-vs-bear asymmetry: 29 / 79 = 0.37x**, against a framework threshold of 2x. At ₹7,872 in July this ratio was 1.88x; at ₹5,010 in June it was materially better. Every rupee of the run has made the bet worse, and the bull case now has to be fully right just to deliver 29%.

The bull case is no longer absurd. It requires the TNT plant to commission, clear its licensing, win defence or export offtake, and carry the mix from 7.5% to 12% margins — a genuine possibility rather than a fantasy, which is the honest change from the June and July notes. It is also entirely in the price and then some.

**A note on why the bull case is not modelled higher.** The EIA scope — 4,000 MTPA TNT, 1,000 MTPA RDX/HMX, and five munition lines at 100,000 units a year each — describes a company several times the size of the one in the table. Deliberately, none of that is modelled. It is unapproved (no EC), unfinanced beyond the ₹163 Cr facility, unlicensed (no MoD or TNT-specific PESO/DIPP status found), absent from every rating document, and absent from management commentary. If the EC issues and the munitions lines get financed and licensed, this stops being the same company and the note gets rebuilt from scratch rather than nudged. Underwriting that outcome today, at 54x, on an application, would be paying for a business that does not yet legally exist.

**Verdict: AVOID stands, at 0% position, on both lenses.** The re-underwrite conditions are in the Action section — the short version is that the TNT plant has to commission and show up in the segment disclosure with a margin, and ICRA's non-cooperation tag has to be resolved, before a 52x multiple has anything to stand on.

## Deep-research update (2026-08-09) — the real change is an ICRA credit downgrade to BB+/Issuer-Not-Cooperating; still no order book

The change to capture is a **credit-rating action, not an order book.** ICRA's report dated **February 23, 2026** downgraded Keltech's ₹120.69 Cr of bank facilities three notches from [ICRA]BBB+ (Stable) to **[ICRA]BB+ (Stable)** and moved them to the **"Issuer Not Cooperating"** category. My first pass on this update chased the "order book" recollection and missed the credit report — this section corrects that. On the order-book question the finding stands: there is no order book, no order-win filing, no defence contract, and no new segment. The BSE announcement stream through the 7 Aug AGM is routine (Q1 results, the 49th AGM, a ₹1.50 dividend, a director reappointment, a cost auditor), and the company had earlier filed a formal clarification to the exchange that nothing undisclosed was behind the stock surge. The "order book" recollection most likely came from Solar Industries, whose defence and SECL explosives orders dominate every Keltech search result.

Three things genuinely changed, and the credit downgrade is the one that matters most:

**0. ICRA downgraded the rating to below investment grade and flagged non-cooperation (23 Feb 2026).** The cut from BBB+ (reaffirmed as recently as 28 Jan 2025) to BB+ was driven not by any deterioration in the numbers — the audited FY25 metrics ICRA still had are healthy (debt/EBITDA 1.0x, interest coverage 8.8x, low gearing) — but by the company ceasing to share performance information and *not paying the surveillance fee despite repeated reminders*. So the downgrade is a governance/opacity signal, not a solvency one, and it corroborates the exact concern the note already carried (no concall, undisclosed capex): a third party has now crossed the investment-grade line to say it cannot get information out of this management. The report also flags the Chowgule family Memorandum of Family Settlement (Jan 2021, still under arbitral-tribunal execution) under which "the Promoters Shareholding of KEL may undergo changes" — an ownership overhang on top of the 54.33% promoter stake. Full detail folded into Concerns and Risks below.

**1. Q1 FY27 (Jun-26) broke the revenue range but not the margin.** Revenue was ₹183 Cr, up 27.7% YoY and the first quarter above the ₹117-143 Cr band the business held for seven quarters. But OPM stayed at 7.5%, PAT fell 1.6% YoY to ₹8.08 Cr, and pre-tax profit rose only 2.3% on that 28% revenue jump. The growth is low-margin volume with no operating leverage — the opposite of what a defence-energetics mix would produce. The old note line claiming "no acceleration is visible" is now corrected in the quarterly table: revenue accelerated, earnings did not.

**2. The FY26 annual report — the binary catalyst the bull case was gated on — was released into the 7 Aug AGM and disclosed no defence segment.** The reporting segments remain Explosives and Perlite. The event the ₹5,010→₹9,750 re-rating was implicitly pricing arrived and confirmed the negative. For a reflexive, thin-float thematic name, a catalyst that resolves *against* the narrative removes the fuel rather than adding it.

**Fundamental verdict — unchanged, and now better-evidenced: AVOID.** At ₹9,750 the stock is ~37x trailing earnings on a 7.5%-OPM commercial-explosives-and-perlite business whose own FY26 AR confirms there is no defence energetics line. The re-rating is complete without the fundamentals ever arriving.

**Swing-trade read (the second lens from the lesson note below) — no fresh entry, and the setup has decayed.** The earlier lesson was that a thin-float thematic name with a *pending* binary catalyst deserved a small speculative trade with a stop and a target. That window is now closed: the catalyst (the AR) has passed with a negative result, the price sits ₹9,750 against a 52-week high of ₹9,864 (essentially at the top after a ~95% run), retail holders thinned from 6,724 to 6,244 through the rally rather than accumulating, and Q1 gave the theme no margin validation to feed on. Chasing here is buying the exhausted end of a reflexive move into a resolved catalyst — poor short-horizon asymmetry. A swing trade needed the entry *before* the run, not at the high after the news. If the price mean-reverts hard toward the ₹5,000-6,000 zone on a theme cooldown, a fresh thin-float setup could be reassessed then; at ₹9,750 there is neither a compounding case nor a swing case.

## Lesson note (2026-08-09) — the AVOID was fundamentally right and still missed a tradeable double

Price went ₹5,010 (June 1 call) → ₹7,872 (July 25) → ₹9,750 (Aug 7). A near-double in ten weeks with no defence order, no defence segment, no concall — the exact fundamental event the bull case was gated on never arrived. The stock re-rated on theme + thin float alone.

What the note got right: the business read. At 37x P/E on a single-digit-OPM commercial explosives company, Keltech is *more* overvalued now, not less. Nothing about the fundamental AVOID for a long-term compounder was wrong, and buying here on the fundamentals would still be a mistake.

What the framework missed, honestly:

1. **Wrong lens for the setup.** This framework prices durable compounding via DCF. Keltech's driver was reflexive, not fundamental: ₹500 Cr cap, ~6,700 holders, ~100% retail float, a hot defence-energetics theme, and a pending binary catalyst (FY26 AR). That combination re-rates on flows, and the note itself said so (see "What success itself triggers" — it literally predicted "prints ₹9,000 within a year"). The DCF was right about the business and irrelevant to the 3-month price path.

2. **The probability weight was miscalibrated.** The note assigned the bull case 15% and let the 2x asymmetry rule (bull-vs-bear 1.88x) veto any participation. But the re-rating did not *need* the fundamental event — it only needed the theme to stay hot and the float to stay thin over a few weeks. Over a 3-month window that is a much higher probability than 15%. I under-weighted reflexivity and float mechanics.

3. **No swing-trade module.** The user is partly a swing trader (exits ~3 months on target). "AVOID as a compounder" is not "AVOID as a swing trade." A thin-float name on a live theme with a catalyst window is precisely the swing setup — it warranted a small speculative position with a hard stop and a target, sized off the 3-month asymmetry, not a blanket zero. The framework has no lens for this, so it defaulted to zero and called the momentum "not a source of information." For a swing trade, price + float + theme *are* the information.

**Fix logged:** when a name is a thin-float thematic momentum play with a near-term binary catalyst, run a separate swing-trade read alongside the compounding read — small speculative size, defined stop, defined target, exit on catalyst or stop. Do not let the long-horizon AVOID suppress a bounded short-horizon trade. This does not change the compounding-framework AVOID; it adds a second answer for the trader the user actually is.

### Revised valuation math at ₹7,872 (up from ₹5,010)

The June 1 note built the base case at ₹6,400 (16x on FY29E EPS ₹400). At CMP ₹7,872, we're already 23% *above* base-case fair value. The bear case remains ₹3,185 (-60% from CMP).

**Asymmetry at ₹7,872:**
- Upside if base case right: (₹6,400 − ₹7,872) / ₹7,872 = **−19%** (negative upside — CMP is above fair value)
- Downside if bear case right: (₹7,872 − ₹3,185) / ₹7,872 = **−60%**
- **Asymmetry ratio: literally negative** — you are paid to sell, not to hold

For a bull-case (₹16,800 in the original note, requiring defence disclosure + 30x re-rating), upside at CMP = (₹16,800 − ₹7,872) / ₹7,872 = **+113%**. Bull-vs-bear ratio = 113/60 = **1.88x** — still fails the framework 2x threshold.

The June call was a wait-for-₹3,500 alert with a defence-disclosure condition. That condition has not been met. Instead the price has run *away* from the trigger, not toward it. The AVOID call stays and gets stronger, not weaker.

## Summary Verdict

### Why this business?

Keltech makes industrial explosives — packaged emulsion cartridges, bulk emulsion, MMAN solution, and the detonators-and-cords accessories that go with them — for India's open-cast mining and cement industry. The customer book on its own website lists Coal India, NMDC, SAIL, Neyveli Lignite, Kudremukh Iron Ore, and roughly a dozen cement majors from ACC and Birla through Shree and Dalmia. Alongside the explosives plants there is a smaller perlite business (~12% of revenue) selling cryogenic insulation media for LNG tanks, filter aid for breweries and pharma, and horticulture substrates. The Chowgule group holds 54% of the equity and has owned this company since it was incorporated as Karnataka Explosives in 1977. What the broader market has done in the last 18 months — and what makes this stock interesting and dangerous in the same breath — is decide that Keltech is a defence energetics proxy. Solar Industries went from 18% to 27% defence mix in FY26 and now sits at a ₹21,300 Cr order book; Premier Explosives carries a 92% defence backlog. Both stocks are at 70-100x P/E. Keltech, by contrast, trades at 19x. The bet would be that Keltech is a cheaper, undiscovered version of the same defence energetics story. The bet I see in the financials is something different: a single-digit-OPM commercial explosives company whose multiple has re-rated on association rather than on disclosed defence revenue.

### Strengths

- **Real customer book in commercial explosives.** Coal India, NMDC, SAIL, Hindustan Zinc, Neyveli Lignite and a dozen cement majors are listed on Keltech's own customer page — a 45+ year operational track record at the unglamorous end of mining services. That is a real moat against new entrants in commercial explosives even if it doesn't translate to defence.
- **Promoter holding stable at 54.33%, no pledge disclosed.** Chowgule group has held this company since 1977 incorporation. Skin in the game is comfortably above the 40% framework threshold, and stable across the last four quarters.
- **Margin direction is positive, off a depressed base.** Operating margin moved from 6% in FY15, troughed at 4% in FY22 when the bulk-emulsion segment was loss-leading, and has crept back to 8% in FY26 (₹41 Cr operating profit on ₹532 Cr revenue). ROCE improved from 15% to 21% over the same period.
- **Working capital is genuinely clean.** Debtor 42 days, inventory 49 days, payables 75 days, cash conversion cycle 16 days — this is unusually disciplined for a company selling to PSUs and cement on the receivables side. It explains why a 7-8% OPM business still throws off ₹48 Cr of OCF in FY26.
- **CWIP build of ₹38 Cr in FY26 (vs ₹9 Cr in FY25, ₹1 Cr in prior years).** The first material capex cycle in a decade is underway. Fixed assets jumped from ₹68 Cr to ₹117 Cr and borrowings climbed from ₹39 Cr to ₹72 Cr to fund it. If — and only if — the capex turns out to be a defence energetics line, the thesis improves materially. Right now management has not disclosed what it is.

### Concerns

- **A TNT plant exists in the accounts and nowhere else in the company's communication (updated 2026-08-20).** The FY26 capital-work-in-progress note names a "TNT Project at Garamsur, Surjagarh Silo set-up", and note 13(a) carries a dedicated Canara Bank term loan "Towards TNT Project" at 8.50%, drawn to ₹20.45 Cr from nil. TNT is a military explosive, so this is the first hard evidence that a defence-grade line is being built. It appears in neither the segment report, nor the Management Discussion and Analysis, nor any announcement, presentation or call. A shareholder can only learn about the company's most valuation-relevant project by reading a one-line footnote to a fixed-asset schedule. That is the concern as much as the project is the opportunity: with no capacity, no commissioning date, no licensing status and no offtake disclosed, the market is paying a defence multiple for something it cannot size.
- **The defence narrative is still not in the financials that matter.** Keltech's own customer page lists zero MoD, Ordnance Factory, DRDO, BDL or PSU defence OEM customers — only mining and cement. The segment report discloses two segments: "Explosives" and "Perlite", with no defence sub-disclosure. Solar Industries discloses defence as 27% of FY26 sales (₹2,634 Cr) and Premier discloses 92% of order book as defence — both are explicit. Keltech is implicit, which usually means it's not material.
- **Single-digit operating margin in a defence-rerated multiple.** OPM 8% in FY26 vs Solar's 28% and Premier's 10% (on a defence-heavy book that just got Liquidated-Damages hit). Defence energetics — propellants, warhead fills, missile-grade RDX — carry 25-35% gross margins because they're qualification-locked single-source supplies. A company with 8% OPM is selling commodity explosives at commodity margins. The narrative and the P&L disagree.
- **Balance sheet leveraged fast, and the approved headroom is far larger than anything explained (updated 2026-08-20).** Borrowings nearly doubled from ₹39 Cr (FY25) to ₹72 Cr (FY26), or ₹79 Cr including current maturities; CWIP went from ₹9 Cr to ₹38 Cr and fixed assets from ₹68 Cr to ₹117 Cr. The purpose is now partly known from the CWIP footnote, but the scale is not: the auditor made the capex a Key Audit Matter (₹55.41 Cr put to use, ₹38.01 Cr in construction, ₹39.96 Cr contracted), and on 7 August the AGM raised the borrowing ceiling to **₹400 Cr** — five times current debt and 2.6x equity — with no project announced to justify it. There is still no investor presentation, no concall transcript and no capex announcement filing.
- **Reported FY26 profit includes ₹3.18 Cr of non-cash write-back (updated 2026-08-20).** Note 40 shows the exceptional item is a reversal of anti-dumping duty on ammonium nitrate imports charged back in FY2020-21, recognised after an April-2026 CESTAT ruling and booked as a "Duty Receivable" rather than cash. Underlying FY26 PAT is about ₹26.3 Cr against ₹28.66 Cr reported, which lifts the multiple at ₹14,250 from about 49x to roughly 54x. The underlying dispute also shows the DRI once pursued the company over its principal raw material, with the Commissioner of Customs levying ₹13.75 Cr in FY2024-25 before Keltech won on appeal.
- **3-year sales CAGR is negative (-2%) despite the run-up.** The 5-year sales CAGR of 19% is the FY22 cartridge-pricing recovery base effect. From FY23 peak revenue of ₹563 Cr, revenue fell to ₹449 Cr (FY24) and has crawled back to ₹532 Cr (FY26). Stock price 3-year CAGR is 44% — pure multiple expansion, not earnings power growth.
- **No analyst coverage, no concall transcript, no investor presentation on the website.** This is a Chowgule group company with the disclosure culture of a closely-held private business. Compare to Solar (quarterly transcripts, sell-side coverage, capital markets day) or even Premier (regular Q&A on order book composition). The information asymmetry favours the promoter, not minority shareholders.
- **The ICRA downgrade was a mandate switch, not a credit event — corrected 2026-08-23, and this materially softens the 9 August reading.** ICRA cut the ₹120.69 Cr facilities from [ICRA]BBB+ to [ICRA]BB+ and tagged them "Issuer Not Cooperating" on 23 Feb 2026, citing unpaid surveillance fees and lack of information. What the earlier pass missed is that **CRISIL assigned BBB+/Stable and A2 to ₹258 Cr of Keltech's facilities on 13 February 2026, ten days earlier**, disclosed to BSE under Regulation 30 with the rating letter annexed and still outstanding today. The ICRA rating is a stale orphan on facilities the new agency now covers at a higher grade. Credit quality is therefore better than the 9 August note implied, and the ₹163 Cr Canara Bank term loan inside that ₹258 Cr is the best-sourced measure of the sanctioned capex envelope, against roughly ₹49 Cr of long-term debt drawn at 31 March 2026. Two residues survive the correction: the company let a live rating decay to BB+ INC rather than pay a fee or formally withdraw it, which is a housekeeping signal; and CRISIL's rating *rationale* could not be retrieved, so **no obtainable rating document from either agency mentions TNT, munitions or defence** — the agencies appear not to have been told, or not to have commented. The Chowgule family Memorandum of Family Settlement (signed Jan 2021, still under arbitral-tribunal execution) remains a separate overhang, under which "the Promoters Shareholding of KEL may undergo changes."
- **The defence capacities are applied for, not cleared (added 2026-08-23).** The MoEF&CC file seeks 4,000 MTPA TNT against an existing sanction that permits only 60 MT of storage, plus 1,000 MTPA of RDX/HMX/HNS, nitrocellulose, nitroglycerine, and five finished-munition lines at 100,000 units a year each. The Terms of Reference were granted in September 2024 and the public hearing was held on 17 May 2025, but **no final environmental clearance is on the public record** and no EAC appraisal minutes after the hearing could be located. Until an EC issues, none of those capacities can lawfully operate. There is also no MoD licence, contract, order or offtake anywhere in the record, and no TNT-specific PESO or DIPP licence status was found — the only DIPP licence referenced is for the detonator plant.

Reinvestment rate is roughly 60% (FY26 OCF ₹48 Cr, net of ~₹28 Cr dividend-and-tax adjusted retentions) at incremental ROCE that has averaged 20% over the most recent 3 years — implies sustainable earnings growth in the 12-13% range, not the 25-30% the multiple needs. Full math in Section 3.

### Second-Order Stress Test

**5-Whys on the ROIC engine:**

> Why does Keltech earn ROCE in the low 20s today?
> 1. Because margins lifted from 4% (FY22) to 8% (FY26) on a recovering revenue base.
> 2. Why did margins lift? → Bulk emulsion pricing recovered as PSU mining volumes normalised post-COVID and ammonium nitrate input costs stabilised.
> 3. Why did pricing recover? → Coal India and the cement industry have a finite organised supplier base for explosives — Solar, Premier, Keltech, IDL Industries (Gulf Oil) and a handful of regional players. When demand recovers, organised supply gets priced.
> 4. Why is the organised base narrow? → Industrial explosives manufacturing is licensed by PESO (Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation). Licences are slow to issue, plants are siting-restricted, and the regulatory cost of compliance keeps unorganised entry low. So the rents are real but they are PESO-licence rents, not technology rents.
> 5. Why does that matter for the thesis? → The fifth answer points at a regulatory moat that is *industry-wide*, not company-specific. Solar has the same moat at 27% OPM with R&D spend, defence qualification and export reach. Keltech has the same moat at 8% OPM with no R&D programme disclosed, no defence qualifications listed, and a customer book dominated by domestic mining PSUs that are themselves margin-pressured buyers. The fifth answer is therefore: Keltech earns ROCE 21% by being one of several PESO-licensed mid-tier players in a recovering commodity cycle — not by being a structurally advantaged business.

If that reading is right, the durable ROCE through a normal cycle is closer to 15-17% than to 21%, and the right earnings multiple is 13-15x, not 19x.

**The world in 2 years if base case plays out:**

By FY28 Keltech does roughly ₹680 Cr of revenue at 9% OPM, PAT around ₹40 Cr, EPS around ₹400. The ₹38 Cr CWIP commissions and unlocks another 15-20% volume capacity. Borrowings probably sit around ₹100 Cr, D/E creeps to 0.6x — still manageable. The new agenda management has to execute on that they haven't done before is twofold: get the capex line operating at the projected margin (no prior-cycle proof to anchor this — the prior decade saw zero meaningful capex), and decide whether to start disclosing defence revenue if any of the energetics narrative is real. The Chowgule group's disclosure culture suggests they will under-communicate either way.

**What success itself triggers:**

Success here would mean the multiple holds at 19-20x while earnings grow 15%-ish — a 15-18% CAGR total return. The risk that creates is that the moment the company genuinely posts disclosed defence revenue, the market re-rates to 35-40x in a single quarter — and the stock that I avoided at ₹5,010 prints ₹9,000 within a year before I have a chance to participate. That is the "missed opportunity" cost of this avoid call. The opposite risk is more material on a fundamentals reading: the next bulk-emulsion pricing trough — and these are cyclical — finds the company carrying ₹100 Cr+ of borrowings against earnings that have collapsed to ₹15 Cr, and the multiple compresses from 19x to 10x at the same time as earnings halve. That combination prints ₹1,500-2,000.

## Deep Dives

### A. The defence-narrative test — is Keltech actually a defence stock?

This is the load-bearing question for the entire thesis, so it deserves a separate walk-through.

The market story is that commercial explosives players in India are pivoting to defence energetics — propellants for rockets and missiles (Pinaka, BrahMos, ASTRA), warhead fills (RDX, HMX-based compositions), and electronic fuzes — and the listed proxies for this trade are Solar Industries, Premier Explosives, and (increasingly) Keltech. Premier Explosives' own filings describe the company as "India's first private sector company to set up an integrated facility for Defence products like High Energy Materials, Propellants for rockets, Warheads and Rockets." That sentence has been quietly attributed to Keltech in some retail-investor commentary, but it does not come from Keltech's own materials.

What Keltech's own materials say is the following. The corporate website (keltechenergies.com) lists no defence customers, no defence products, no integrated energetics facility. The dealer-marketing website (keltechenergies.co.in/our-customer.html) shows a customer roster of Coal India, NMDC, SAIL, Neyveli Lignite, Kudremukh Iron Ore, Hutti Gold Mines, ONGC, and roughly a dozen cement majors — and zero MoD/Ordnance Factory/DRDO/BDL entries. The segment report in FY26 quarterly filings discloses two segments — Explosives and Perlite — with no defence sub-line. The Q4 FY26 results outcome filed with BSE on May 27, 2026 makes no reference to defence revenue or orders. There is no investor presentation on the website that names a defence product.

The Manuguru plant in Khammam district, Telangana is sometimes cited as the "defence plant." Manuguru is in fact Keltech's bulk emulsion plant serving Singareni Collieries (the Telangana state-owned coal miner) and the surrounding Coal India mines — the same plant that has been there for years.

The conclusion I draw, which is a falsifiable conclusion the FY26 annual report can refute, is that Keltech is a commercial explosives company with negligible defence revenue today and no disclosed defence capex programme. The market is pricing 18-19x P/E on what it hopes will become a defence story; the company's own disclosure shows what it actually is. If FY26 annual report discloses a defence sub-segment, this view flips immediately. Until then the burden of proof is on the bull.

### B. Why Solar Industries trades at 5x Keltech's multiple — the structural margin gap

A common retail framing is "Keltech is the cheap version of Solar." Let me set the math down so the framing fails honestly. Solar generates ₹9,838 Cr of revenue at 28% OPM and 31.5% ROE; Keltech generates ₹532 Cr of revenue at 8% OPM and 19% ROE. The Solar Industries margin profile is not a function of being bigger — Solar at ₹500 Cr revenue in FY12 was already running 18-20% OPM. The structural difference is product mix and R&D investment.

Solar runs three explosive lines that Keltech does not: (1) detonators, electronic detonators, and detonating cords manufactured in-house — high-margin precision components; (2) defence energetics — solid propellants for Pinaka, warhead fills, primary explosives — which Solar has been delivering and disclosing for over a decade; (3) export markets — Solar derives roughly 35% of revenue from overseas operations in Turkey, Australia, Zambia and a dozen others, where pricing is significantly above Indian PSU rates. Keltech is essentially a single-line bulk-and-cartridge emulsion business serving Indian PSU mines, with a small perlite side-business that has nothing to do with explosives.

So the multiple gap is not a discount waiting to close. It reflects a real, persistent business-quality gap. Closing that gap requires Keltech to build what Solar built over 20 years — detonator capability, defence qualification, export markets — and there is no evidence in the current capex or capability disclosure that this is what the ₹38 Cr CWIP funds.

### Q1. Is incremental ROIC genuinely high (>20%) — and is it structural, not cyclical?

Keltech's ROCE has improved from a depressed 9-12% range in FY20-21 to 20-26% in FY24-25 (and dipped back to 21% in FY26 on the capex investment). On the surface that is multi-bagger-quality return on capital. The honest read is that the 3-year incremental ROIC calculation runs on a low base: NOPAT in FY23 was roughly ₹14 Cr (PAT ₹12 Cr + after-tax interest ~₹2 Cr) against capital employed of ~₹110 Cr; FY26 NOPAT is ~₹35 Cr against capital employed ~₹220 Cr. Incremental ROIC = (35 − 14) / (220 − 110) = 19%, just below the 20% multi-bagger threshold. That is real, but it was earned during a period when bulk emulsion prices were recovering and PSU mining volumes were normalising — the cyclical lift.

The structural test: does Keltech sell anything that earns rents? PESO licences and plant siting are real barriers but they are industry-wide; the same licence protects Solar Industries (28% OPM), Premier Explosives (10% OPM with defence book), Gulf Oil Lubricants/IDL (mid-teens OPM), Deepak Fertilisers' explosives division. Keltech at 8% OPM is at the low end of the licensed-supplier band, which suggests it earns the licence rent but captures none of the product-differentiation rent that Solar earns through detonators, defence qualifications, and exports. The implication is that through-cycle ROCE is more likely 14-17% than 20-21%, and the recent print is the peak of a cycle that already happened.

---

### Q2. How much capital can be redeployed at these returns — what's the reinvestment runway?

Indian industrial explosives consumption tracks coal production (Coal India produced ~830 MT in FY25, growing to ~1,000 MT by FY28 per government plans), iron ore (NMDC and SAIL), copper and zinc (Hindustan Zinc), and cement (which is itself growing 7-8%). That underlying market is real but slow — a 6-8% CAGR end-market. Keltech's 12-yr revenue CAGR of 9% is essentially in line, suggesting it has been holding market share, not gaining.

Reinvestment math: Keltech retained roughly ₹28 Cr of FY26 PAT after the ₹1.50 dividend (negligible payout), and deployed ₹75 Cr into capex during the year (the gap funded by ₹33 Cr of fresh debt). At a steady-state 20% ROCE on the incremental ₹75 Cr, this would unlock ₹15 Cr of additional EBIT — roughly a 25% lift on FY26 EBIT of ₹41 Cr. That timeline is 18-24 months from commissioning.

The runway is short, however. The PESO licensing system caps how fast a single player can add capacity without triggering siting controversies (the Vishwasnagar plant has had local opposition in the past). Beyond two capex rounds, organic growth is rate-limited by coal mining and cement industry growth — 7-8%. The reinvestment-rate × ROIC math therefore caps at roughly (60% reinvestment × 20% ROIC) = 12% sustainable growth, and the 3-7 year runway sits in the "possible 2-3 bagger" band of Phase 2.4, not the multi-bagger band.

### Q3. What's the implied compounding math?

The math, expressed plainly: if Keltech retains 60% of earnings (mostly funded into working capital and capex) and earns 20% incremental ROCE on that retained capital, intrinsic value compounds at 12% per year. Over 5 years that produces 1.76x of intrinsic-value growth. If the multiple holds at 17-18x (no re-rating), total return is also 1.76x — a 12% CAGR. If the multiple compresses to 12-13x as the cycle turns or as the defence narrative fails to validate, total return is closer to 1.2-1.3x = 4-6% CAGR. If, conversely, defence orders materialise and the multiple re-rates to 30x à la Solar/Premier, the same 12% earnings growth produces 3x in 5 years.

Entry multiple 19x → base case exit 17x (modest de-rating as cyclicality and undisclosed capex pressure margins for 1-2 years) → **total return 1.3x in 3 years** in the base case = 9% CAGR.

**Smell test:** A 9% base-case CAGR is below my opportunity cost in Indian equities (12-13% nominal expected). The math itself is sustainable — 12% earnings growth on 20% ROCE is genuinely durable for 5-7 years given the PESO moat — but it doesn't clear the multi-bagger bar.

### Q4. What breaks the thesis?

**The compounding breaks if:** Bulk emulsion ammonium nitrate pricing rolls over (it's softened modestly in 2026 vs the 2022-23 cycle high) while the ₹38 Cr CWIP commissions into a soft market and the leverage taken to fund it carries 9-10% interest. The combination is OPM compressing from 8% to 5%, EBIT falling from ₹41 Cr to ₹25 Cr, interest cost rising from ₹4 Cr to ₹8 Cr, PAT collapsing to ₹13-15 Cr — a 50% decline from FY26 print. **Leading indicator I'd see before the stock price tells me:** Quarterly OPM tracking. If two consecutive quarters print OPM below 6.5%, the cyclical down-leg is starting; that's the time to confirm capex commissioning is into a weak market rather than a strong one.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

### Market Position

Indian industrial explosives is a moderately fragmented but PESO-protected market with five-six organised players controlling ~80% of legitimate supply. Market size is roughly ₹12,000-15,000 Cr depending on the year; Coal India alone procures ~₹3,000 Cr+ of explosives annually through competitive tenders. Solar Industries is the disclosed market leader with roughly 25-30% market share on the commercial side and dominance in defence. Premier Explosives is the defence-focused player. Keltech is a mid-tier supplier, likely 3-4% market share by volume, concentrated in PSU mining contracts in southern and central India.

### Structural Advantages vs Peers

1. **Long-tenure PSU relationships:** 45+ years supplying Coal India and the South-India mining majors. Switching costs in PSU tenders are low (price-led), but qualification and tender empanelment are real. Quantified effect: working capital cycle of -1 day vs Premier's ~60+ days suggests stronger payment terms or product mix (cash sales at site magazines vs longer-tenor government orders).
2. **Working capital discipline:** Cash conversion cycle of 16 days vs sector average of 40-60 days, achieved through tight inventory and longer payable cycle (75 days). Effect: lower working capital intensity allows higher capital turnover; partly compensates for thin operating margin.
3. **PESO licence + plant siting at Manuguru:** Bulk emulsion delivered ex-magazine to large coal mines is a logistically constrained business — the plant near the mine wins. Manuguru (near Singareni and Coal India's Telangana operations) is well-sited. Effect: regional captive customer position, defensible against new entrants.

## Glossary — Key Industry Terms

**Industrial / commercial explosives**
The bulk products used in coal, iron ore, copper and cement mining to break rock — packaged cartridge explosives (sticks wrapped in plastic, loaded by hand into drilled holes) and bulk emulsion explosives (a viscous water-in-oil emulsion of ammonium nitrate that is pumped directly into the borehole at the mine site). The economics are commodity-like — the active ingredient is ammonium nitrate at roughly 70% of cost, the margins are in formulation, accessory bundling, and proximity to the customer mine.

**Bulk emulsion**
A specific type of explosive made by emulsifying an oxidiser solution (ammonium nitrate + water + emulsifier) inside a fuel oil. Insensitive in transport, sensitised at the borehole by adding gas bubbles. Lower margin than cartridge but higher volume — sold by the tonne, delivered ex-magazine or pumped on-site.

**MMAN (mono-methyl-amine nitrate)**
A sensitiser / coupling agent used in emulsion explosives. Keltech makes this as a building block for its own emulsion line and sells some to third parties.

**PESO (Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation)**
The Indian regulator that licenses the manufacture, transport, storage, and use of explosives. Licences are site-specific, slow to issue, and the regulatory cost of compliance keeps unorganised manufacturers out of the legitimate market. PESO licences are the industry-wide moat for organised explosives players.

**Defence energetics / high-energy materials**
Specialty explosives and propellants used in military applications — solid rocket propellants (composite propellants based on HTPB binder + ammonium perchlorate oxidiser + aluminium fuel; or double-base propellants based on nitrocellulose + nitroglycerine), warhead fills (RDX, HMX, TNT, and composition mixtures), and primary explosives for fuzes (lead azide, lead styphnate). These carry 25-35% gross margins because they are single-source-qualified for specific weapon programmes (Pinaka, BrahMos, ASTRA, Akash) and customers cannot switch supplier without re-qualifying the whole programme.

**Solid propellant**
The fuel that powers a solid-rocket motor. For tactical missiles like Pinaka and ASTRA, solid propellants are the standard because they are storable, instantly ignitable, and don't require pumps. Premier Explosives is the principal listed Indian private-sector manufacturer.

**Perlite**
A volcanic glass that expands 10-20x when heated to ~900°C, producing a lightweight, fire-retardant, low-thermal-conductivity material. Used as cryogenic insulation in LNG tanks and pipes, as a filter aid for breweries and pharma, and as a horticulture growing medium. Keltech's perlite business is ~12% of revenue and serves all three end-uses.

**PESO defence-class licence**
A separate licence category required to manufacture defence-grade explosives (as distinct from commercial mining explosives). Solar Industries holds this licence; whether Keltech does is not disclosed in any of its public materials.

**Ind AS 108**
The Indian Accounting Standard requiring segment disclosure where a single segment exceeds 10% of revenue or assets. Keltech discloses two segments — Explosives and Perlite. If defence revenue exists, it is bundled inside Explosives at below-10% materiality.

**CESTAT**
Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal — the appellate body for indirect tax disputes. Keltech recognised a ₹3.18 Cr exceptional gain in FY26 from a favourable CESTAT order on anti-dumping duty refund.
